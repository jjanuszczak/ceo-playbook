## LinkedIn Candidates

### Option 1

Some AI valuations look insane because investors are asking the wrong first question.

The question is not only: what is the current revenue multiple?

The better question is: what is the company learning that competitors cannot cheaply copy, and how fast does that learning compound?

That is the idea behind Learning Capital:

- Models
- Proprietary datasets
- Evaluation libraries
- Workflow memory
- Agent systems
- Human feedback loops
- Organizational cognition

The asset is not the model alone.

The asset is the loop: deploy, observe, learn, improve, redeploy.

So What? AI diligence should measure learning rate, not just ARR. A wrapper sells access. A Learning Capital company gets harder to copy every time the product is used.

https://januszczak.org/articles/learning-capital/?utm_campaign=thought-leadership&utm_medium=social&utm_source=linkedin

### Option 2

Most AI companies are not Learning Capital companies.

A thin interface on top of a general model is not Learning Capital.

A token reseller is not Learning Capital.

A services firm manually fixing AI output behind the curtain is not Learning Capital.

The test is sharper:

Does the system accumulate proprietary, reusable intelligence as it operates?

If every customer interaction, correction, edge case, and workflow trace makes the product more reliable or harder to copy, the company may be building a compounding asset.

If not, it is probably just arbitraging the current model release cycle.

So What? Investors should stop underwriting "AI exposure" and start underwriting learning economics.

https://januszczak.org/articles/learning-capital/?utm_campaign=thought-leadership&utm_medium=social&utm_source=linkedin

### Option 3

DCF and revenue multiples are not wrong.

They are incomplete when the most important asset is being created off balance sheet and improves through use.

In AI, some of today's spend on compute, engineering, data operations, and customer implementation is not just expense.

It can become:

- Proprietary data
- Workflow memory
- Evaluation systems
- Agent capability
- Lower future cost to serve
- Higher switching cost
- Better product velocity

That does not justify every AI valuation. Many will fail.

But it explains why the old asset map is weak.

So What? The scarce resource is no longer just money, compute, or labor. It is the ability to turn money into intelligence that improves faster than the money is consumed.

https://januszczak.org/articles/learning-capital/?utm_campaign=thought-leadership&utm_medium=social&utm_source=linkedin
