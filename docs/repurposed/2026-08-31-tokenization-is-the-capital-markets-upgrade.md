## LinkedIn Candidates

### Option 1

Tokenization is usually framed as a crypto story.

I think that framing is too narrow.

The stronger argument is market infrastructure:

- Ownership records are fragmented.
- Settlement is still delayed.
- Asset servicing is still operationally heavy.
- Collateral does not move as efficiently as it should.
- Cash and asset legs often sit on different rails.

Tokenization does not magically make an asset better. A tokenized security is still a security.

But it can move the ownership record, transfer logic, compliance controls, and settlement workflow closer together.

So What? The executive question is not "Should we launch a token?" It is "Which expensive market workflow becomes cheaper, faster, or safer if ownership and cash can move on programmable rails?"

https://januszczak.org/articles/tokenization-is-the-capital-markets-upgrade/?utm_campaign=thought-leadership&utm_medium=social&utm_source=linkedin

### Option 2

Debt will likely move before equities in tokenized capital markets.

That is not because debt is simple. It is because the pain is more concrete:

- Coupon payments
- Collateral mobility
- Repo workflows
- Private credit administration
- Fund subscriptions and redemptions
- Cross-border settlement

Equities bring a harder political and operational stack: shareholder rights, proxy voting, transfer agents, broker entitlements, securities lending, exchange rules, and retail protection.

Debt can teach the operating discipline. Equities will test whether the industry has learned it.

So What? The migration path matters more than the TAM slide.

https://januszczak.org/articles/tokenization-is-the-capital-markets-upgrade/?utm_campaign=thought-leadership&utm_medium=social&utm_source=linkedin

### Option 3

The best argument against tokenization is not that it is useless.

The best argument is that intermediaries exist for real reasons.

They absorb risk, enforce rules, correct errors, handle fraud, comply with sanctions, provide custody, and maintain order when markets break.

Tokenization will not remove that trust layer. It will force the trust layer to modernize.

That is why the likely winners sit in the middle:

- Regulated enough to be trusted.
- Technical enough to move faster than the old stack.
- Commercial enough to pull issuers and investors into the network.

So What? Tokenization is not the end of financial institutions. It is a test of which institutions can turn trust into programmable infrastructure.
