## LinkedIn candidates

### Option 1

Ask an LLM to pick a random number from 1 to 100 and you may get a highly repeatable answer.

That is not a novelty. It is a production-design lesson.

Language models generate likely continuations. They do not automatically provide the properties a workflow may require:

- Uniform sampling
- Unique identifiers
- Cryptographically secure tokens
- Reproducible test data

Use the model at design time to help specify, write, test, and review the implementation. At runtime, execute the reviewed component that can prove the property you need.

Takeaway: fluent output is not the same as a verified system result.

https://januszczak.org/articles/your-ai-just-picked-47-again/?utm_campaign=thought-leadership&utm_medium=social&utm_source=linkedin

### Option 2

The strongest enterprise AI pattern is a boundary, not a prompt.

Let the model accelerate the work: drafting code, proposing tests, explaining edge cases, and reviewing implementation options.

Then put the critical behavior into normal software that your team can inspect, version, test, monitor, and replace.

That distinction separates a useful pilot from a production system.

Takeaway: use AI to build the tool. Do not quietly make the model the tool when the job requires a guarantee.
