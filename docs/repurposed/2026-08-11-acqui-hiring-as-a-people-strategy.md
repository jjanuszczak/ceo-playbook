## LinkedIn Candidates

### LinkedIn Option 1

Acqui-hiring is no longer just a soft landing for struggling startups.

In AI and other specialist markets, it has become a leadership strategy.

The real asset is not only talent. It is coordination:

- Shared language
- Trust under pressure
- Product taste
- Technical context
- Decision speed
- A team that already knows how to work together

That is hard to recruit one person at a time.

So What?

The deal only works if leaders protect what they bought. Give the incoming team a real mandate, explicit decision rights, operational support, and a retention plan built around scope and impact, not just money.

### LinkedIn Option 2

The biggest mistake in an acqui-hire is treating acquired entrepreneurs like normal senior hires.

They usually bring high agency, speed, customer closeness, and product instinct.

They may also underweight documentation, process, cross-functional coordination, and operating hygiene.

That is not a bug. It is the predictable tradeoff in the talent profile.

So What?

Do not buy entrepreneurial energy and then punish it for not behaving like a corporate staff function. Design the operating container: clear ownership, complementary operators, retention metrics, and explicit decision rights.

### LinkedIn Option 3

Acqui-hiring is capital allocation, not HR administration.

The buyer is making a judgment about scarcity:

- Is this team faster than recruiting?
- Is its trust already formed?
- Does it own scarce technical judgment?
- Can it restart a stalled product line?
- Can the host company absorb its speed?

The last question is the one executives skip.

So What?

Integration is not a post-close chore. It is the deal thesis. If the company cannot give the team ownership, status, and operating support, it should probably not buy the team.
