## LinkedIn

### Option 1

The evidence does not support the easy claim that AI is killing book reading.

Pew’s latest survey still finds broad book readership. The real concern is not whether people can buy books. It is whether they still do the reading, synthesis, and verification that produce independent judgment.

For leaders, the operating rule is simple: use AI after you have encountered the primary material on high-stakes work.

The takeaway: AI can make a team faster. It should not become an excuse to make the team shallower.

### Option 2

Book sales are a poor proxy for attention.

They measure purchases. They do not tell you whether someone finished a book, understood the argument, or changed a decision because of it.

The more useful management question is whether your team has protected enough uninterrupted time to read, write, and challenge the default answer before a model supplies one.

The takeaway: protect depth as an operating capability, not a personal wellness ritual.

### Option 3

Generative AI changes the cost of producing an answer. It does not remove the cost of understanding the question.

For board decisions, investments, and regulatory changes, I would require three things from AI-assisted work:

- The primary sources
- The counterargument
- The uncertainty that remains

The takeaway: “the model said so” is not analysis. It is a prompt to begin analysis.
