## LinkedIn Candidates

### Option 1

Most online trends are not independent signals.

They are often one original post, screenshot, chart, or clip turned into hundreds of quote posts, replies, summaries, and rewrites.

That matters for creators and builders because the platform rewards adjacency to momentum more than ownership of the relationship.

The practical response is not to abandon platforms. They still help with discovery.

The response is to rebuild the follow as infrastructure:

- Publish somewhere you control.
- Expose a feed.
- Build an email list.
- Make the canonical source easy to find.
- Use AI to help readers filter noise, not to replace the original relationship.

So What? A follower count is not the same as distribution. If the platform controls allocation, part of the audience is rented.

https://januszczak.org/articles/the-restatement-loop/?utm_campaign=thought-leadership&utm_medium=social&utm_source=linkedin

### Option 2

RSS sounds old because it is boring.

That is exactly why it still matters.

A site publishes. A reader checks. New items appear. The reader chose the sources. The creator keeps the publishing endpoint. Nobody has to pretend every reading decision is a growth-hacking opportunity.

The modern version should not be a nostalgic copy of Google Reader. It should add AI at the reader's edge:

- Group duplicate reactions.
- Summarize chosen sources.
- Translate and brief.
- Point back to the original.
- Keep subscriptions portable.

So What? The future of reading does not need fewer algorithms. It needs more user-owned intent.

https://januszczak.org/articles/the-restatement-loop/?utm_campaign=thought-leadership&utm_medium=social&utm_source=linkedin

### Option 3

AI makes the feed problem sharper.

It can turn one viral post into ten rewritten takes, twenty summaries, and a hundred synthetic replies. That makes derivative commentary cheaper and faster.

But the same capability can make personal reading better.

The question is where the AI sits.

If it sits inside the platform, it can become another ranking layer.

If it sits with the reader, it can identify duplicates, brief trusted sources, and help the reader get closer to the origin of an idea.

So What? AI is not automatically a savior or a villain. It is an accelerant. The operating design decides whether it amplifies noise or protects judgment.
