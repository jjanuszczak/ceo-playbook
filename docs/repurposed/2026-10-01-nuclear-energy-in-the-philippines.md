## LinkedIn Candidates

### Option 1

RA 12305 is a serious step for Philippine nuclear energy.

But it is not a cheap-electricity law.

It creates PhilATOM, an independent nuclear regulator responsible for safety, security, safeguards, licensing, inspections, enforcement, and related regulatory control.

That matters. Nuclear deployment needs a credible regulator.

But the commercial gates remain:

- Financing
- Liability
- Procurement
- Grid readiness
- Waste and decommissioning
- Workforce depth
- Public trust
- Fuel dependency
- Construction risk

So What? Nuclear should stay on the table, but it has to earn its place against renewables, storage, gas, transmission, and efficiency on full system cost and delivery risk.

### Option 2

The Philippines does not need a nuclear slogan.

It needs a nuclear gate process.

BNPP rehabilitation, a new large reactor, and small modular reactors are three very different bets. Each has a different risk profile.

BNPP has site familiarity, but also age, seismic, modernization, trust, and cost questions.

New-build nuclear has scale, but large capex, construction-delay risk, and tariff exposure.

SMRs may fit staged deployment, but commercial maturity and licensing precedent still matter.

So What? The right question is not whether nuclear is good or bad. The right question is whether a specific nuclear pathway beats the alternatives after financing, grid, waste, liability, and governance are priced honestly.

https://januszczak.org/articles/nuclear-energy-in-the-philippines/?utm_campaign=thought-leadership&utm_medium=social&utm_source=linkedin

### Option 3

Philippine electricity cost is now a competitiveness issue.

That is why nuclear is back in the conversation.

But high power prices do not automatically make nuclear viable. They only raise the urgency of comparing all options honestly:

- Renewables plus storage
- Gas transition capacity
- Transmission upgrades
- Demand response
- Efficiency
- BNPP rehabilitation
- New-build nuclear
- Small modular reactors

Nuclear may reduce long-run system costs if procured and financed well.

It may raise costs first if guarantees, construction risk, waste, or decommissioning are hidden.

So What? Regulatory readiness is necessary. Commercial viability still has to be proven.
