# Editorial Notes

## Brief and intended reader

- **Issue:** [#186](https://github.com/jjanuszczak/ceo-playbook/issues/186) (`Editorial: SQLLite for version control`)
- **Target reader:** Intermediate-to-advanced engineers, systems programmers, and OS/storage enthusiasts who have seen the "SQLite is 35% faster than the filesystem" claim and want architectural context, caveats, and practical takeaways.
- **Core thesis:** SQLite's small-blob speed and space advantages (~10 KB and under) are real, but replacing general-purpose filesystems with SQL or maintaining dual-copy arbitration layers backfires. BeFS succeeded by adding B+tree-indexed attributes to a real filesystem; WinFS failed by layering a relational engine over NTFS; Git's packfiles already collapse the bulk of the small-file problem, leaving loose objects and refs as the primary candidates for single-file stores.

## Research basis and citations

- SQLite official benchmark: ["35% Faster Than The Filesystem"](https://sqlite.org/fasterthanfs.html) (~35% faster read/write on ~10 KB blobs, ~20% less space, open/close syscall amortization).
- SQLite documentation: ["Internal Versus External BLOBs"](https://sqlite.org/intern-v-extern-blob.html) (~100 KB crossover rule of thumb).
- Zerodha Blobasaur (`https://github.com/zerodha/blobasaur`): production sharded SQLite + `zstd` blob store, independent sub-1 KB read benchmarks showing 2.5x to 3.3x gains over raw filesystem reads.
- Dominic Giampaolo, *Practical File System Design with the Be File System* (`https://archive.org/details/fsdesign`) and Haiku (`https://www.haiku-os.org/`) OpenBFS continuation.
- WinFS history: pulled from Windows Vista in 2004 and canceled in June 2006 due to CPU/memory/complexity overhead layering SQL Server over NTFS.
- Fossil SCM: ["101 Reasons Why Fossil Is Better Than Git"](https://fossil-scm.org/home/doc/trunk/www/hundredandone.md) (all-SQLite repository with zlib + delta compression).
